Finding a foundation shade involves both visible skin tone and undertone.

## Skin Tone vs Undertone

Skin tone describes the visible depth of your complexion. Undertone refers to the underlying tendency often described as warm, cool or neutral.

## Identifying Undertone

Use several observations rather than relying on a single test. Popular shortcuts such as looking only at vein colour can offer clues for some people but should not be treated as universally reliable.

## Where Should You Test Foundation?

Compare a small number of promising shades around the jawline and look for the one that blends most naturally between the face and neck.

## Give the Formula Time

Some formulations can look slightly different after settling or drying. Allow a little time before making a final judgement.

## Check the Lighting

Artificial lighting can affect colour perception. Where practical, examine the match in natural light as well.

## Shade Is Not Coverage

Shade matching and coverage are separate decisions. See [**Foundation Coverage Guide**](guide:NVBG-005).

## Virtual Try-On

Virtual try-on can help narrow options and preview shades, but camera quality, lighting, display settings and the underlying technology can influence the result.

Treat it as a decision aid rather than a guarantee of an exact physical match.

## Summary

Compare a few likely shades around the jawline, consider undertone, allow the formula to settle and check the result under appropriate lighting.
