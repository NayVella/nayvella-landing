Understanding your skin is the starting point for building a suitable care routine. A product that feels comfortable on dry skin may not suit skin that produces more oil, and skin needs can change with climate, age, routine and other individual factors.

It is also useful to distinguish between **skin type** and a temporary skin condition. Oily skin, for example, can still experience periods of dehydration or sensitivity.

## The Main Skin Types

Skin is commonly described as oily, dry, combination or normal. Sensitivity can occur alongside different skin types, so it is often more useful to consider it a characteristic or condition rather than a completely separate type.

### Oily Skin

Oily skin tends to produce more noticeable sebum.

You may notice:
- visible shine during the day;
- more noticeable pores in some areas;
- oil returning relatively quickly after cleansing;
- a tendency toward congestion or blemishes for some people.

Oil itself is not inherently negative. Sebum plays a natural role in the skin. The goal is not to remove every trace of oil, but to cleanse and care for the skin without unnecessarily stripping it.

### Dry Skin

Dry skin may feel tight or less comfortable, particularly after cleansing or exposure to dry air.

You may notice:
- tightness;
- rough or flaky areas;
- less visible shine;
- a frequent need for hydration.

Air conditioning and dry environments can make these sensations more noticeable.

### Combination Skin

Combination skin shows different characteristics in different areas.

A common pattern is:
- more oil through the forehead, nose and chin;
- cheeks that are normal or drier;
- different care needs across the face.

This means the entire face does not always need to be treated identically.

### Normal Skin

Normal skin is generally relatively balanced between oiliness and dryness.

It may feel comfortable most of the time, show neither pronounced oiliness nor dryness, and require less continuous management of a single dominant concern.

Normal skin still benefits from cleansing, hydration and daytime sun protection.

## What About Sensitive Skin?

Sensitive skin may react more readily to certain products or environmental factors.

Possible signs include redness, stinging, burning, itching or general discomfort.

These symptoms can have many causes. Persistent, significant or concerning irritation is better assessed by an appropriately qualified healthcare professional rather than self-diagnosed from a beauty guide.

## A Simple Way to Observe Your Skin

For a general indication:

1. Wash your face with a gentle daily cleanser.
2. Pat it dry.
3. Do not immediately apply skincare products.
4. Wait for a short period and observe how the skin feels and looks.

Noticeable shine across most of the face may suggest an oily tendency. Tightness or dryness may suggest a dry tendency. Oil concentrated in the T-zone with normal or drier areas elsewhere may suggest combination skin. Skin that remains relatively comfortable and balanced may fall closer to normal.

This is a **general at-home observation, not a diagnostic test**, and a single day is not always enough to classify your skin confidently.

## Why Skin Behaviour Can Change

Skin can respond differently over time because of factors such as heat and humidity, sun exposure, air conditioning and dry air, seasonal changes, age, product use, cleansing habits and individual factors.

Observe your skin rather than treating one label as permanently fixed.

## Skin Type Is Not the Same as a Skin Condition

Skin type describes broad characteristics such as oiliness or dryness.

Persistent acne, inflammation, unusual pigmentation, rash or other skin changes may require a different type of assessment.

Oily skin, for example, does not mean every concern that appears on the skin is caused by oil.

## What Should You Do After Identifying Your Skin Type?

Start with a simple routine:

1. suitable cleansing;
2. appropriate hydration;
3. daytime sun protection.

Additional steps can be considered gradually.

If your skin tends to be oily, see the [**Oily Skin Routine**](guide:NVBG-002).

For daytime protection, see [**How to Choose the Right Sunscreen**](guide:NVBG-003).

## Summary

Identifying your skin type is useful because it helps you understand your skin's needs.

Observe oiliness, dryness, differences between facial areas and how your skin responds to products and the environment. Skin needs can change, and your routine can adapt with them.
